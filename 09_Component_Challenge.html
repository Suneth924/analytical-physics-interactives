Why do we need coordinates and vectors?

Saying that an object is “3 meters away” does not tell another person where to find it. The direction matters too. A coordinate system gives everyone the same origin and the same positive and negative directions, so positions and motion can be described consistently.

A vector has both magnitude and direction. Its components tell how much of the vector lies along each chosen coordinate axis.

What to notice

  • A component’s sign comes from its direction relative to the corresponding positive axis—not from where it happens to appear on the page.
  • A zero component means there is no displacement along that coordinate direction.
  • Adding a dimension adds a component: r = <x>, then r = <x,y>, then r = <x,y,z>.
  • The vector r includes direction. Its magnitude |r| is a nonnegative distance and has no direction.

Position and velocity notation

r = <x,y,z> = x i + y j + z k

v = <vx,vy,vz> = vx i + vy j + vz k

Velocity is a vector. Speed is the magnitude of velocity, written |v|. Velocity and speed are not the same physical quantity.
