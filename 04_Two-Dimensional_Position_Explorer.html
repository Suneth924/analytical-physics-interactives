Vectors and coordinate systems

Two-Dimensional Position Explorer

A position is not fully described by a distance alone. We also need a direction. A coordinate system gives everyone the same origin and directions, so positions and motion can be described consistently.

📍

Saying “the object is 5 metres away” does not tell us where it is. Saying “4 metres to the right and 3 metres upward from the origin” identifies one position. A vector combines this magnitude and direction information.

1-D: one direction 2-D: two components 3-D: three components
1

Begin in one dimension

A number line introduces position, sign, and direction.

Physical concept An object can be to the right or left of a chosen origin.
Mathematical representation r = ⟨x⟩. The sign of x specifies direction.
Numerical example r = ⟨−3⟩ m.
Physical interpretation The object is 3 m left of the origin. Its position magnitude is |r| = 3 m.

What to notice

  • The coordinates x and y are the horizontal and vertical components of the position vector.
  • The dashed projections form a right triangle with the position vector.
  • The vector r includes direction; its magnitude |r| is only the straight-line distance from the origin.
  • Changing a component can change both the magnitude and the direction of the vector.
3

Extend the idea to three dimensions

A third perpendicular direction describes height or depth.

Physical concept A point in a room needs left–right, forward–back, and up–down information.
Mathematical representation r = ⟨x, y, z⟩ = x i + y j + z k.
Numerical example r = ⟨2, −3, 6⟩ m and |r| = √(22 + (−3)2 + 62) = 7 m.
Physical interpretation The point is 2 m along +x, 3 m along −y, and 6 m along +z. Its straight-line distance from the origin is 7 m.

The same language describes velocity

v = ⟨vx, vy, vz⟩ = vx i + vy j + vz k

Velocity is a vector because it has magnitude and direction. The magnitude |v| is the speed. Velocity and speed are therefore not the same quantity.

Coordinate choices may change the component numbers, but they give observers a shared way to describe the same physical location.
