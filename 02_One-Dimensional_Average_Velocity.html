One-Dimensional Average Velocity

Connect position, signed displacement, elapsed time, and the direction of average velocity.

Why do we need coordinates and vectors?

Saying “the object is 4 metres away” is incomplete unless everyone agrees on a reference point and a positive direction. A coordinate system provides that shared reference. It lets different observers describe positions, directions, and motion consistently.

Some physical quantities have only a magnitude. Others, such as position and velocity, also have direction. A quantity with both magnitude and direction is a vector.

Coordinates answer “where and in which direction?” Vectors use those coordinates to describe directed physical quantities.

From one dimension to three dimensions

1-D: motion along a line

Physical concept

One coordinate locates an object. Positive and negative signs indicate opposite directions.

Visual diagram

Mathematical representation

r = <x>

Numerical example

If x = −3 m, then r = <−3 m>.

Physical interpretation

The object is 3 m from the origin in the negative x direction.

2-D: motion on a surface

Physical concept

Two coordinates are needed to locate an object on a floor, map, or screen.

Visual diagram

x y

Mathematical representation

r = <x,y> = x i + y j

Numerical example

r = <3 m, 4 m>. Its magnitude is √(3² + 4²) = 5 m.

Physical interpretation

Move 3 m in x and 4 m in y; the straight-line distance from the origin is 5 m.

3-D: motion in space

Physical concept

A third coordinate describes height or depth as well as length and width.

Visual diagram

x y z

Mathematical representation

r = <x,y,z> = x i + y j + z k

Numerical example

r = <2 m, 3 m, 6 m>, with magnitude √(2² + 3² + 6²) = 7 m.

Physical interpretation

The three components describe one position vector, not three separate objects.

A velocity vector may be written v = <vx,vy,vz> = vx i + vy j + vz k. The vector v includes direction; its magnitude |v| does not have a direction. The magnitude of instantaneous velocity is speed. Velocity and speed are therefore not interchangeable words.

What to notice

  • Displacement is signed: Δx = xf − xi. It depends only on the endpoints.
  • A negative position does not automatically mean negative velocity. The sign of average velocity depends on how position changes.
  • Because elapsed time is positive, displacement and average velocity have the same sign.
  • vavg is a directed quantity. |vavg| is its nonnegative magnitude.
  • The animation shows one constant-rate trip consistent with the selected endpoints. Endpoints alone do not reveal every detail of the actual motion between them.
  • If an object reverses direction, the magnitude of average velocity is not generally the same as average speed.
