Magnitude Challenge

Build intuition for vectors and coordinate systems, then use rectangular components and the Pythagorean theorem to find vector magnitudes.

Why do we need coordinate systems?

Saying that an object is “5 meters away” is not enough to locate it. Is it east, west, above, or below us? Physical quantities such as position and velocity can have direction. A coordinate system gives everyone the same origin and the same positive directions, so positions, directions, and motion can be described consistently.

A vector has both magnitude and direction. Its magnitude is a nonnegative scalar that tells only the vector’s size.

From one dimension to three dimensions

1-D: One number line

  1. Physical conceptMotion is restricted to one line. A sign distinguishes the two directions.
  2. Visual diagramChoose an origin, a positive direction, and a negative direction on a number line.
  3. Mathematical representationr = <x>. The component may be positive or negative.
  4. Numerical exampler = <−4> m, while |r| = 4 m.
  5. Physical interpretationThe object is 4 m from the origin on the negative side. The vector and its magnitude are not the same.

2-D: A plane

  1. Physical conceptTwo perpendicular directions are needed to locate an object on a floor or map.
  2. Visual diagramThe x- and y-components form the legs of a right triangle. The vector is the hypotenuse.
  3. Mathematical representationr = <x,y> = x i + y j.
  4. Numerical exampler = <3,4> m, so |r| = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m.
  5. Physical interpretationThe position is 3 m in x and 4 m in y. Its straight-line distance from the origin is 5 m.

3-D: Physical space

  1. Physical conceptA third perpendicular direction describes height or depth as well as horizontal position.
  2. Visual diagramFirst combine x and y into a horizontal diagonal. Then combine that diagonal with z using another right triangle.
  3. Mathematical representationr = <x,y,z> = x i + y j + z k. Also, v = <vx,vy,vz> = vxi + vyj + vzk.
  4. Numerical exampler = <2,3,6> m, so |r| = √(2² + 3² + 6²) = 7 m.
  5. Physical interpretationThe three components describe direction. The single number 7 m is only the vector’s magnitude.

What to notice

  • Components depend on the chosen coordinate directions, but together they describe one vector.
  • In 2-D, the magnitude is the hypotenuse of a right triangle.
  • In 3-D, use the Pythagorean theorem twice: combine x and y, then include z.
  • A velocity vector v includes direction. Its magnitude |v| is the object’s speed.
  • A position magnitude |r| is straight-line distance from the chosen origin; it is not necessarily distance traveled.
