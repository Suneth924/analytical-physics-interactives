Vectors and Coordinate Systems

Two-Dimensional Vector Component Explorer

Physical quantities such as position and velocity can include a direction. A coordinate system gives everyone the same reference for describing where an object is and how it is moving. A vector combines a magnitude with a direction.

Why coordinates?

“The cart is moving at 5 m/s” gives a speed but not a direction. “The cart is moving at 5 m/s to the right” is more useful, but we must first agree which way is positive. Labeled axes provide that common reference.

Why vectors?

A single number cannot completely describe directed motion. Velocity is a vector: it tells us both how fast and which way. Its magnitude |v| is the object's speed.

1

One dimension

Concept: Motion is restricted to one straight line.

Representation: r = <x>

Example: r = <4 m> means 4 m on the positive side of the origin.

Interpretation: The sign communicates which side or direction.

2

Two dimensions

Concept: Motion can have horizontal and vertical parts at the same time.

Visual: Use two perpendicular axes, labeled x and y.

Representation: r = <x, y>

Example: r = <3 m, 4 m>

Interpretation: One pair of components identifies one position vector.

3

Three dimensions

Concept: A third perpendicular direction describes depth or height.

Visual: Add a z-axis perpendicular to the x and y directions.

Representation: r = <x, y, z>

Example: r = <2 m, 3 m, 1 m>

Interpretation: Three numbers locate an object in space.

From picture to mathematics

  1. Physical concept: The object has one velocity, even though we describe it using two parts.
  2. Visual diagram: The horizontal and vertical arrows are perpendicular. Placing them tip-to-tail reaches the tip of the resultant.
  3. Mathematical representation: v = <3, 4> m/s.
  4. Numerical example: Use the Pythagorean theorem because the components form a right triangle.
|v| = √(vx² + vy²) = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m/s

Physical interpretation: The object moves right and up. Its speed is 5 m/s.

What do i and j mean?

The symbols i and j are unit vectors: direction markers of length 1.

i points in the positive x-direction
j points in the positive y-direction

Therefore, v = vx i + vy j. The numbers multiplying i and j tell how much velocity lies along each axis. A negative coefficient points opposite to that axis's positive direction.

Vector versus magnitude: v includes direction, while |v| is a nonnegative number and is the speed.

What to notice

  • Two perpendicular components describe one resultant vector; they are not three separate motions.
  • Changing a component's sign reverses that part of the direction without making the speed negative.
  • If one component is zero, the resultant lies on an axis. If both are zero, the object is at rest and the zero vector has no direction.
  • Larger component magnitudes generally produce a longer resultant and a greater speed.
  • The same coordinate agreement lets different observers describe the motion consistently.
Looking ahead

The same idea in three dimensions

Add a third perpendicular component: v = <vx, vy, vz>.

In unit-vector notation: v = vx i + vy j + vz k, where k marks the positive z-direction.

Example: v = <2, 3, 6> m/s has |v| = √(2² + 3² + 6²) = 7 m/s. This is the same Pythagorean idea extended to three perpendicular directions.

Coordinate choices organize our description; they do not change the actual motion.
