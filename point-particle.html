What the point model keeps

Position of a chosen point, usually the center of mass.

Whole-object quantities such as total mass, velocity, and net force can still be assigned to that point.

What the point model leaves out

Orientation, shape, surface contact, deformation, and variation from one side of the object to the other.

What to notice

  • A point-particle model does not claim that the real object has zero size. It says size is not needed for the current question.
  • Reducing D/L usually strengthens the model because the object occupies a smaller fraction of the relevant scale.
  • If the surrounding field changes across the object, D/Lfield also matters.
  • Required accuracy matters: a ratio acceptable for a rough estimate may be unacceptable for precision work.
  • Rotation, tides, contact, drag, and internal structure can make size or shape essential even when the geometric ratio is small.
Simple classroom test
Scale burden = larger of D/L and D/Lfield
Budget use = scale burden ÷ required fractional accuracy

This ratio test is a modeling guide, not a universal error formula. A final validity check must return to the physical question and the assumptions.
