Vectors and Coordinate Systems

Three-Dimensional Vector Explorer

Connect three velocity components to one velocity vector, its direction, and its magnitude. Begin with the physical need for coordinates, then progress from one dimension to three.

Why do we need coordinates and vectors?

A number alone is sometimes incomplete

Saying “the building is 300 metres away” gives a size, but not a direction. Different people could walk 300 metres and arrive at different places.

A coordinate system gives everyone the same origin and the same positive directions, so positions and motions can be described consistently.

Motion has direction

A car moving east at 20 m/s and a car moving west at 20 m/s have the same speed, but different velocities.

A vector is a quantity with both magnitude and direction. Its components tell how much of the vector lies along each coordinate axis.

Central idea: coordinates provide the shared reference; a vector uses that reference to communicate magnitude and direction.

Stage 2

Two dimensions: two independent directions

For a flat map, take +x to the right and +y toward the top of the map.

1

Physical concept

A location on a floor or map needs two coordinates. One number cannot distinguish all possible locations.

2

Visual diagram

3

Mathematical representation

r = ⟨x, y⟩

The ordered components must match the agreed axis order.

4

Numerical example

r = ⟨3, 4⟩ m

|r| = √(3² + 4²) = 5 m

5

Physical interpretation

The point is 3 m in +x and 4 m in +y. The straight-line distance from the origin is 5 m.

Making sense of the three-dimensional model

1

Physical concept

Motion through space can include sideways, forward–backward, and upward–downward motion at the same time.

2

Visual diagram

The colored component arrows lie along the axes. The purple arrow combines all three into one velocity vector.

3

Mathematical representation

r = ⟨x, y, z⟩

v = ⟨vx, vy, vz⟩

4

Numerical example

For v = ⟨6, −4, 3⟩ m/s,

|v| = √(6² + (−4)² + 3²) = √61 ≈ 7.81 m/s.

5

Physical interpretation

The signs give axis directions. The magnitude gives speed; it does not replace the directional information in velocity.

Two ways to write the same vector

Component notation

r = ⟨x, y, z⟩ and v = ⟨vx, vy, vz⟩. The order corresponds to x, then y, then z.

Unit-vector notation

r = x i + y j + z k and v = vx i + vy j + vz k. Here i, j, k indicate the positive x, y, and z directions.

Vector versus magnitude

v is velocity, including direction. |v| is its nonnegative magnitude. The magnitude of velocity is speed.

Three-dimensional Pythagorean theorem

|v| = √(vx² + vy² + vz²). This combines perpendicular component lengths into one straight-line length.

What to notice

  • Three component values describe one vector; they are not three separate velocities.
  • A negative component reverses direction along its axis. It does not make the magnitude negative.
  • A zero component has zero arrow length, so the vector lies partly or entirely in a lower-dimensional space.
  • If two components are zero, the vector is effectively one-dimensional. If one is zero, it lies in a plane.
  • Rotating the view changes the drawing on the screen but not the component values, magnitude, or physical vector.
  • The purple arrow shows velocity, not the path already traveled by an object.
